# Outputs proportional to the task

## Topic discovery

Present the profile and assumptions briefly, a varied candidate table, a reasoned shortlist, verification status and next choices. Do not fill a full evidence report with empty or fictional findings.

## Selected topic: Research Design and Roadmap

1. Three or more proposed English titles and a reasoned recommendation.
2. Brief value/feasibility assessment and status of any gap claim.
3. Problem, rationale, aim and main/subquestions; hypotheses only if suitable.
4. Scope, context, unit of analysis and limitations.
5. Method, rationale and alternatives; required access, data, sample/materials and tools.
6. Procedures, analysis, evaluation criteria and uncertainty handling.
7. Verification/validation plan appropriate to the design.
8. Search plan or executed search record, clearly labeled.
9. Deliverable-specific contents, work stages, schedule, risks and fallback.
10. Three to five next actions, with a concrete first step.

Use planned-work wording. Do not automatically add an abstract. If one is explicitly required for a proposal, identify it as a proposal abstract and avoid fabricated findings.

Adapt the contents:
- Article: focused rationale and contribution, methods and a plan for later results/discussion.
- Thesis: institution-specific chapters and degree-appropriate contribution.
- Capstone/design project: need, requirements, alternatives, implementation and evaluation.
- Funding: fit to call, objectives, novelty rationale, methods, work packages, resources, schedule, risks and intended outputs.
- Technical/feasibility report: need, alternatives, calculations, constraints, cost/performance and recommendation.
- Conference submission: actual submission format and word/page limit.

## Full evidence/gap report

Use only sections supported by completed work:
1. Decision summary and scope.
2. Review mode, protocol status and methods.
3. Corpus inventory and access/metadata profile.
4. Searches, dates, screening and reproducibility record.
5. Comparative evidence matrix.
6. Thematic synthesis.
7. Contradictions, null findings and boundary conditions.
8. Evidence quality and justified confidence.
9. Candidate gap register.
10. Counterevidence, nearest studies and adversarial validation.
11. Prioritized questions.
12. Deliverable-appropriate designs.
13. Feasibility, ethics, access and next steps.
14. Limitations.
15. References with verified identifiers/URLs and access status.

Use stable IDs across records; cite sources at the claims they support. Keep a concise readable report while retaining the audit trail.
